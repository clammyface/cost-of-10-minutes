Product Case Study · Indian Quick Commerce

Blinkit's biggest complaint isn't slow delivery. It's the price of speed.

I mined 40,671 real Play Store reviews across four Indian quick-commerce apps. Fees and pricing opacity drive 20.2% of every negative Blinkit review — more than three times the rate of late delivery, which ranks ninth. This is what I found, and what I'd build about it.

01 · The moment it breaks

A ₹98 basket costs ₹152 — and you find out last

Users itemise the fees accurately in their own complaints. The information was never hidden from them. What they object to is when it arrives: after three minutes of shopping, at the one moment they can no longer do anything but cancel.

The grievance is sequence, not amount. That distinction is the whole case study: it means the problem is solvable by product design rather than by cutting prices — which matters enormously, because cutting prices is exactly what Blinkit cannot afford to do.

Blinkit solved the problem it was founded to solve

A company built entirely on ten-minute delivery now sees lateness as its ninth complaint. Speed has become table stakes across the category. The competitive frontier moved to what the speed costs — and nobody's product has followed it there.

03 · Three findings that changed the strategy

The people complaining about fees are your good customers

25.8%

Fee complaints rise as anger falls

25.8% of 3★ reviews mention fees, versus just 14.1% of 1★. The angriest users are dealing with rotten produce and vanished refunds. Fee complaints come from the mildly annoyed.

½×

They mention nothing else

Fee complainers cite every other problem at roughly half the base rate. This isn't part of an "everything is broken" grievance — it's a standalone objection from users whose experience is otherwise fine.

11.0%

Zepto proved it's fixable

Zepto scrapped all handling and surge fees. Its fee complaint rate is 11.0% against Blinkit's 20.2% — same cities, same category, identical sampling method.

Together these reframe the target. The fee complainer is not a churning hater — they're a habituated, profitable customer with exactly one objection, and it's an objection a competitor is actively courting them over. That makes them the most winnable segment in the dataset.

Zepto can give fees away because it's private, loss-making, and buying share with investor capital. Blinkit is listed, newly profitable, and judged quarterly on a margin trajectory it spent five quarters building. It cannot win a fee-elimination war and shouldn't enter one.

The problem, restated

Make fees feel fair, predictable, and controllable — without removing them.

05 · The answer

Smart Cart

Chosen by RICE across 15 candidate solutions. Three components that only work together — and the reason they ship as one feature is the most important design decision in the case study.

See the real total

Live landed cost, itemised, from the first item added — not at checkout. Fixes the sequence.

See the gap

"Add ₹47 more to save ₹50 in fees," with a progress bar. Both numbers stated, always.

Close it usefully

Suggestions ranked from the user's own reorder history — never by margin, which would confirm the opportunism users already resent.

Why not ship the transparency alone?

Because showing someone a higher total with no way to act on it just delivers bad news sooner. That's pure abandonment risk with none of the upside. The modelled launch bears it out: carts that got transparency with a nudge improved by 0.4pp, while carts that got transparency alone worsened by 2.1pp.

The commercial logic is what makes this worth building: helping a user clear a fee threshold means a bigger basket. The user's problem and the AOV problem have the same solution — so nothing has to be traded away, and not a single fee changes.

07 · What this study can't tell you

The limitations, stated up front

The corpus is deliberately skewed

Equal quota per star rating, so 1–3★ are heavily over-represented on purpose — the taxonomy needs negative text to cluster on. Blinkit's real average is 4.58★ across 9.0M ratings. Every figure here is a share of negative reviews, never of users.

Every share is a lower bound

Hand-labelling 40 reviews found 68% received at least one correct theme, with 32% carrying a real complaint no rule caught. Precision is good; recall is the weakness. Because misses spread across themes, the ranking is sturdier than the levels.

Android only

Apple's public review feed now returns zero entries for every app tested; Reddit's JSON endpoints return 403 without OAuth. Both were attempted and both failed — documented rather than quietly dropped.

The launch results are modelled

Smart Cart was never shipped — this is a portfolio study, so document 15's figures are projections from published benchmarks, labelled throughout. Everything upstream of it is real and verifiable.
